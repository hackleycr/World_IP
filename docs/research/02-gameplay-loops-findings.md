# Insight, not grind, keeps sandbox players returning

Across the 58 games in this study, players stay longest when the core loop rewards understanding: a loop that adds new rules to learn keeps going, and a loop that fills a finite power or content bar runs out. Live Steam data pulled on 2026-10-20 for 37 of the games makes this measurable. **The nine systemic/sandbox titles are all rated 93.5–97.9% positive (median 96.7%), and their median 24-hour peak is 21.6% of their all-time peak.** The comparable figure is **7.6% for knowledge/discovery games** and **16.8% for the commercial control group**, and the control figure is propped up by session-reset games such as Counter-Strike 2 (66%) and Dota 2 (70%) ([Steam Charts: Factorio](https://steamcharts.com/app/427520); [Steam Charts: RimWorld](https://steamcharts.com/app/294100); [Steam Charts: Counter-Strike 2](https://steamcharts.com/app/730)). The systemic games with the longest lives (Minecraft, Terraria, Factorio, RimWorld, Satisfactory, Oxygen Not Included) all pair a seconds-long physical verb with goals the player sets. Their concurrent-player records come years after launch, when expansions add new rule sets: **Factorio's 2024 Space Age peak (about 118,000) was 3.4x its 2020 peak** ([GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2024/10/factorio-space-age-sold-over-400000-copies-and-sets-a-new-player-count-record)), and **RimWorld set its all-time record (about 96,000) in July 2025** ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/294100/charts/)) and still draws 35,141 concurrent players ([Steam Charts](https://steamcharts.com/app/294100)). Commercial games built around a power or content ceiling show the opposite pattern. **Palworld's 2026-10-20 player count is 2.3% of its 2.1M peak** ([Steam Charts](https://steamcharts.com/app/1623730)), and **Helldivers 2 lost about 90% of its players in four to five months** ([Stevivor](https://stevivor.com/news/helldivers-2-has-lost-90-of-its-pc-player-base-over-five-months)). The worst-rated games in the study are the ones that devalued player effort: **Diablo IV is "Mixed" at 65.3% positive** and **Destiny 2 is at 78.7%** ([Steam reviews: Diablo IV](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/2344520?json=1&language=all&purchase_type=all&num_per_page=0&filter=all); [Steam reviews: Destiny 2](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/1085660?json=1&language=all&purchase_type=all&num_per_page=0&filter=all)). Players complain about design far more than about bugs: **57% of negative Steam reviews cite design and 17% cite bugs**, and negative reviews are written after a median of only **6.6 hours** ([PC Games Insider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/67005/report-bad-design-is-the-no1-complaint-of-steam-user-review-authors)). The fairness complaints that do the most damage are about devalued effort, not long effort. Players accept long optional grinds, but they turn on a game when nerfs, resets or redesigns erase work they already did. All four frameworks point to the same audience: MDA's Discovery, Quantic Foundry's Creativity and Strategy motivations, Yee's "understanding mechanics" explorer, and self-determination theory's competence and autonomy. For the planned 2D natural-science sandbox, the evidence supports all three pillars, with four conditions. The first insight payoff has to arrive within the first session. Permanent damage has to read as meaningful and caused by the player. Recipes have to work as completed, shareable artifacts. Randomized-rules mode has to keep methods useful while facts change.

## Live Steam data covers 37 games; other figures come from press reports

Read this before any number in the report. The data comes from two tiers. **Tier 1 is live Steam data pulled on 2026-10-20 for 37 games** (in `data/steam-metrics-2026-10.csv`). It has the Steam review summary, total reviews and percent positive from the Steam store `appreviews` API; current players, 24-hour peak and all-time peak from steamcharts.com; and SteamSpy owner ranges. Each row in that file carries its own source URLs. Two cautions apply. **Steam Charts all-time peaks are sampled hourly, so they can sit slightly below SteamDB's records.** CS2 is 1,818,368 on Steam Charts against a press-reported 1,824,989, and Factorio is 118,166 against 118,674. **SteamSpy owner figures are coarse range estimates**: Palworld's "50–100M", for example, is a bucket, not a measurement. SteamSpy playtime fields were empty, so there is no Steam playtime data. Where a live figure exists, this report uses it in preference to older press numbers. Where a press figure records the date of a peak event, the report keeps that date.

**Tier 2 is everything else.** It comes from web-search extracts of press articles, developer statements, and third-party trackers such as SteamPulse, Vaporlens, Raijin, Sensor Tower, Gamalytic and IsThereAnyDeal, and most of it dates from 2024–2025. **SteamDB, HowLongToBeat, Metacritic, Quantic Foundry and Wikipedia pages remained inaccessible** from the research environment. Where a URL points to SteamDB or Wikipedia, the figure was read from a search-result extract of that page, not from a live fetch. Twenty-one games have no live Steam row. Some are not on Steam (Minecraft, the Zelda games, Fortnite, League of Legends, Genshin Impact, Candy Crush, Clash Royale); the others were not in the pull (e.g. The Powder Toy, Sandboxels, Diablo III, EVE Online, Rust). For those games, press figures are the only source. Valve does not publish sales, so every unit figure from Raijin, SteamPulse, Sensor Tower, Gamalytic or Steam Page Analyzer is a model estimate, and these estimates often disagree by 2x. Outer Wilds, for example, is estimated at 1.8M to 3.74M copies ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/753640/Outer_Wilds); [Steam Page Analyzer](https://www.steampageanalyzer.com/tools/revenue-calculator/753640)), while SteamSpy puts it in the 1–2M bucket ([SteamSpy](https://steamspy.com/api.php?request=appdetails&appid=753640)). The live pull also settles one earlier doubt: Noita's SteamPulse all-time peak of 3,045 was wrong, and **Steam Charts records 9,144** ([Steam Charts](https://steamcharts.com/app/881100)).

Two more caveats apply. First, **every loop breakdown (core action → feedback → reward → invest → new capability) is analyst synthesis**, assembled from cited design statements and reviews; none is quoted from a source. The same goes for the MDA and Quantic Foundry tags in the dataset, and those columns should be read that way. Second, the live pull collected aggregate review scores, but not review text. The planned review-mining step (paging through the full review API and topic-tagging with BERTopic or an LLM) was therefore not run. The "grouped and quantified" sentiment analysis below combines three things: the live percent-positive scores, one large published review study (Lin et al.'s 10.95M reviews), and a tally of sourced praise and complaint statements per game. It is not a fresh analysis of review text. The full dataset of 58 games is at `data/gameplay-loops.csv`: 14 systemic/sandbox, 19 knowledge/discovery and 25 commercial control-group titles. It includes every game in the notes that has either a loop breakdown or a game-specific signal, and its cells are left blank wherever the notes had no data. For the 37 games in the live pull, the dataset's `review_signal`, `peak_concurrent` and `sales_or_players` columns now lead with the 2026-10-20 Steam figures. Where Steam Charts gives no date for an all-time peak, the `peak_date` cell says so.

## Durable loops pair a seconds-long verb with goals the player sets

The longest-lived sandbox games share a structure. A tactile action measured in seconds (dig, place, cast, attach) gives instant, visible systemic feedback, and it sits inside a long-term loop with no scripted end. Developers say this outright. Nintendo's Breath of the Wild team described moving from "additive" to "multiplicative" design, in which objects react to the player and to each other through a separate "chemistry engine" of elemental rules. They prototyped it in a 2D remake of the original NES Zelda ([Thumbsticks](https://www.thumbsticks.com/gdc-17-breaking-conventions-breath-of-the-wild); [Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2017/03/09/zelda-team-discusses-breath-of-the-wilds-development-and-2d-zelda-prototype-at-gdc/)). Ludeon presents RimWorld as "a story generator" rather than a game ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1024232/-RimWorld-Contrarian-Ridiculous-and)). Bay 12's manual says "Losing is fun!" and "There is no win condition" ([Jackson Free Press](https://www.jacksonfreepress.com/news/2013/apr/24/joy-failure/)). Noita simulates every pixel as a cellular automaton that follows simple local rules ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/noita-a-game-based-on-falling-sand-simulation/)). That is the same model as the planned MVP's 2D grid.

The three groups split on **where progress is stored**. In systemic games it lives in the world and in reusable player-made artifacts: blueprints, Autobuild schematics, redstone circuits, wand builds. In knowledge games it lives in the player's head. Outer Wilds' creative director says the game is "all knowledge-gated. So if you know everything, you can just boop-da-boop-da-boop" ([Medium interview with Alex Beachum](https://medium.com/@cordialkobold/interview-with-alex-beachum-creative-director-of-outer-wilds-a01bb9631e20)). In the commercial control group it lives either in skill and rank, for session-reset games such as CS2, League of Legends, Dota 2 and Fortnite, or in a power curve with a ceiling, for loot and collection games such as Diablo IV, Destiny 2, Genshin Impact and Palworld. The table below shows representative loops. **All loop cells are synthesis**, and the dominant-effort labels use the pillars doc's four effort types.

| Game (group) | Core action → feedback | Reward → invest | New capability | Time scale | Dominant effort |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Noita (sandbox) | Cast spells in a pixel-simulated world → materials burn, melt, flow, explode | Gold, wands, spells → wand editing | Stranger wand behaviour; deeper descent | Seconds; run ≈ session | Insight + risk |
| Factorio (sandbox) | Place belts, inserters, assemblers → items flow, bottlenecks show | Science packs → research | New machines; blueprints copy solved designs | Seconds; open-ended megabase | Insight, then time |
| Oxygen Not Included (sandbox) | Queue dig/build orders → gas, heat and germ overlays change | O2, power, materials → plumbing, automation | Stable closed-loop systems | Minutes; colony cycles | Insight + risk |
| Tears of the Kingdom (sandbox) | Attach parts with Ultrahand → contraption moves or fails | Zonai devices → saved Autobuild schematics | Larger vehicles, new traversal | Seconds; hours | Insight + creativity |
| Valheim (sandbox) | Gather, fight → drops, trophies | Tiered materials → gear, base | Survive next biome after boss gate | Minutes; biome tiers | Time (grind) + risk |
| The Powder Toy (sandbox) | Brush an element → instant reaction | Discovery or saved creation → share | Bigger contraptions | Seconds | Insight only (no economy) |
| Outer Wilds (knowledge) | Explore in a 22-min loop → ship-log fact added | Facts → spend next loop on a hypothesis | Reach knowledge-gated location | 22-min loop; ~20 h | Insight |
| Baba Is You (knowledge) | Push word blocks → rules re-evaluate, free undo | Solved level → branch choice | Rule-interaction idioms | Seconds; hours per insight | Insight |
| Opus Magnum (knowledge) | Build a machine → simulation pass/fail | Cost/cycles/area metrics → optimize vs histogram | Better technique | Minutes to hours | Insight + optimization |
| Little Alchemy / Infinite Craft (knowledge) | Combine two items → new item or nothing | New element → combine further | Larger frontier | Seconds | Brute force + some insight |
| Counter-Strike 2 (control) | Round of aim and utility → kill, round win | Economy → buy weapons | Round power; rating | 1–2-min round | Skill + insight |
| Balatro (control) | Play a poker hand → chips × mult | Money → jokers | Synergy engine; stake unlocks | 20–40-min run | Insight + risk |

| Palworld (control) | Capture, build → base output | Materials, Pals → tech tree | Automation | ~40–80 h until tree exhausted | Time + light insight |

Six features recur among the long-lived systemic titles (synthesis). The first is the seconds-scale verb with visible feedback. The second is a legible rule set whose rules combine multiplicatively. The third is unlocks that widen the space of possible actions rather than raising numbers. The fourth is player-set goals once scripted goals end. The fifth is reusable artifacts that capture insight and can be shared. The sixth is a long cycle of updates and expansions. Falling-sand toys show what happens with the second feature alone. The Powder Toy is free and rated "Overwhelmingly Positive", and players report thousands of hours, yet its recurring criticism is a "lack of traditional gameplay loops" ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/1148350/the_powder_toy.md); [GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2024/06/classic-free-physics-sandbox-game-the-powder-toy-is-now-on-steam/)). Noita and Oxygen Not Included are the closest commercial proof that a pixel or fluid simulation can carry a full game once goals and pressure are added on top.

Systemic games re-peak late, and the trigger is new rules rather than more numbers. Factorio's Space Age expansion added planets with their own mechanics and produced a peak 3.4x its 2020 high. Satisfactory's 1.0 release produced a 3.1x jump ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/09/11/satisfactory-peaks-at-over-100k-ccu-launch)). Steam Charts now shows a record of 185,957, so a later peak beat even that one; the pull does not record when it happened ([Steam Charts](https://steamcharts.com/app/526870)). RimWorld's record coincided with the Odyssey DLC ([SteamDB](https://steamdb.info/app/294100/charts/)), and on 2026-10-20 the game still drew 37.5% of that record in its 24-hour peak, along with Oxygen Not Included (39.2%) the strongest long tail in the sandbox group ([Steam Charts](https://steamcharts.com/app/294100)). The control group shows the mirror image. **Counter-Strike 2 broke its franchise record in its 13th franchise year** ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/counter-strike-2-keeps-breaking-its-own-peak-player-record)), because every match resets players to zero and progress lives in skill. Palworld, Helldivers 2 and Destiny 2 each shed 90–97% of their peaks within months. Destiny 2's case adds a cause: The Final Shape "gave players a perfect exit point" by ending a decade-long saga, and the director admitted the Edge of Fate progression changes "sounded great on paper but didn't work" ([Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/destiny-2s-latest-expansion-failed-to-meet-player-expectations-director-admits-1586995)). Seasonal resets in Diablo IV turn the spike into a sawtooth rather than flattening it. Season 4 set a Steam high of 39,782 ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/?p=24899)), and a fan wiki describes heavy play at launch followed by decline well before the season ends ([GuildOrder](https://guildorder.com/games/diablo4/wiki/the-seasonal-reset-cycle)). Pocketpair responded to Palworld's −97% by saying "it's normal to take a break from a game" ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20240215-27211/)), which amounts to accepting that a finite loop is finite. The causal story, that expansions which add rule sets bring lapsed players back more than numeric content does, is **an inference from the timing of peaks and is not proven**. Marketing, price and platform events also move these curves.

Industry baselines put these numbers in context. GameAnalytics' 2025 mobile benchmarks across about 11,600 apps found a median D1 of about 22%, D7 under 4% and D30 about 0.7%, with top-decile games at roughly 40% / 11–12% / 4%. Retention fell from 2024 ([GameDevReports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)). The 2026 edition added PC for the first time, but its figures were not extracted ([InvestGame PDF](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-27-2026-mobile-pc-benchmarks_compressed.pdf)). On PC, ownership overstates engagement. Ars Technica's sample of about 250,000 profiles found about 26.5% of purchased games never played after correction ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2014/04/16/over-one-third-of-purchased-steam-games-have-never-been-played)), and GameDiscoverCo puts the average unplayed share of a library at 32.7% ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-many-pc-games-get-bought-but-not-played)). Completion is low even for acclaimed games: **26.6% of Elden Ring players and 23.7% of Witcher 3 players earned the main-story achievement**, and the median game is finished by about 36% ([Den of Geek](https://www.denofgeek.com/?p=824046)). Only a minority of owners will ever reach a sandbox's late-game emergent systems, so the early hours have to carry the case. No published D1/D7/D30 curve was found for any game in this study, which is the largest quantitative gap.

## Players reward and punish design, not polish

The largest published study of Steam reviews is Lin, Bezemer, Zou and Hassan in *Empirical Software Engineering* (2018), covering about 10.95M reviews of 6,224 games snapshotted in March 2016 ([Unpaywall DOI record](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2FS10664-018-9627-4); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/deep-study-of-10-million-steam-reviews-is-absolutely-fa-1825908713)). It found that **about 34% of all reviews and 57% of negative reviews mention game-design problems, against 8% and 17% for bugs** ([PC Games Insider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/67005/report-bad-design-is-the-no1-complaint-of-steam-user-review-authors); [CogConnected](https://cogconnected.com/2018/05/study-gamers-shown-care-poor-design-bugs/)). Negative reviews came after a median of **6.6 hours** of play and positive ones after **15.5 hours** ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/steam-bad-reviews-study)). Early Access reviews ran longer and more positive ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/deep-study-of-10-million-steam-reviews-is-absolutely-fa-1825908713)). The data is ten years old, and a 2024 VR study found no link between polarity and playtime, so the 6.6-hour figure is a guide rather than a law. Its implication still matters for a systemic game: the verdict on the loop forms in roughly the first evening of play.

The live ratings line up with the themes below. At the top of the 37-game pull are games whose effort turns into understanding or lasting output: Stardew Valley 98.5%, Vampire Survivors 98.3%, Hades 98.0%, RimWorld 97.9%, Balatro 97.8%, Baba Is You 97.7%, Factorio 97.5% ([Steam reviews: Stardew Valley](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/413150?json=1&language=all&purchase_type=all&num_per_page=0&filter=all); [Steam reviews: Factorio](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/427520?json=1&language=all&purchase_type=all&num_per_page=0&filter=all)). At the bottom are the games most associated with devalued effort, grind or friction, and the most opaque discovery games: Diablo IV 65.3%, Helldivers 2 75.5%, Destiny 2 78.7%, Cultist Simulator 79.9% ([Steam reviews: Helldivers 2](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/553850?json=1&language=all&purchase_type=all&num_per_page=0&filter=all); [Steam reviews: Cultist Simulator](https://store.steampowered.com/appreviews/718670?json=1&language=all&purchase_type=all&num_per_page=0&filter=all)). Without review text, these scores cannot be attributed to specific themes, so the link is suggestive rather than demonstrated.

Factorio sits on both sides of the ledger. Reviewers call line-building "a wonderful puzzle" whose running "is hugely gratifying" ([Windows Central](https://windowscentral.com/factorio-pc-review)), and IGN, via an aggregator, calls the game "dauntingly complex" ([GameGeeker](https://gamegeeker.com/games/factorio/review?country=US)). That points to the most common failure mode for insight-heavy games: **the onboarding cliff, not the grind**. Alexis Kennedy put it this way: early Cultist Simulator builds were "so opaque as to be almost unplayable", and "confused players lose patience very quickly" ([Kennedy, "The Way through the Wood"](https://medium.com/@alexiskennedy/alexis-kennedy-on-cultist-simulator-the-way-through-the-wood-part-5-df3fc2459780)). Arvi Teikari found that, as a solo developer, he became "blind" to difficulty, so puzzles he judged too easy proved very hard ([MCV/DEVELOP](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/)). Lin et al.'s finding that design complaints dominate fits this: a systemic game is far more exposed to "I don't understand why that happened" than to crashes.

## Players accept long grind but revolt when earned progress is taken away

The evidence on reward-per-effort points to a sharper rule than "less grind". Players **accept very long effort when it is optional and permanent, and revolt when past effort is devalued**. A Diablo IV forum thread defends Paragon 300 as "grindy on purpose", since "normal players aren't meant to reach" it in a season ([Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2344520/discussions/0/597393727567221195)). The same game drew three weeks of complaints and a hotfix when Season 14 made earlier best-in-slot Mythics "weaker and harder to acquire" ([DiabloBytes](https://www.diablobytes.com/diablo-iv/news/)). Destiny 2's progression redesign is the expensive version of the same mistake. Mismatches in the other direction also break perceived fairness: one streamer cleared a tier marketed as a "weeks-long grind" in about 17 hours ([DiabloBytes](https://www.diablobytes.com/diablo-iv/news/2026-04-29-diablo-4-streamer-pulverizes-new-lord-of-hatred/)).

Within sandboxes, the praised designs **convert grind into insight through automation**: once a player understands a system, they stop doing the work by hand. Factorio's blueprints and TotK's Autobuild are the clearest cases. Valheim's tier-gated material gathering is the counterexample, called "excessively grindy and tedious, especially after the initial biomes" ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/892970/valheim.md)), though some players argue it is only grindy "if you want fully upgraded gear" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/892970/discussions/0/3494256256414828268)). Two failure modes sit at the edges (synthesis). Grind without insight burns players out. Insight without goals leaves them drifting, which is The Powder Toy's critique and the Factorio forum's complaint that megabases offer "no new problems" ([Factorio forums](https://forums.factorio.com/viewtopic.php?p=536611)). Randomness that controls access to the expressive toolkit is resented. Noita's GOG reviewers complain that progress "depends on how lucky you are to find new wands and spells rather than your own skill" ([GOG](https://www.gog.com/game/noita)), and a Steam player calls the early game a "slog" without a good wand ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/881100/discussions/0/3159763879055769749)).

Roguelite meta-progression shows how to weight persistent rewards. Slay the Spire's unlocks are "very limited… quickly exhausted and basically work as a tutorial" ([lemm.ee](https://next.lemm.ee/comment/14156553)). Balatro's unlocks add breadth (jokers, decks, stakes), not stats. That insight-first design sold more than 5 million copies as a solo-developer game, by one source's count ([GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/publishing/poker-roguelike-balatro-sells-more-than-five-million-copies-311dcc58f214ad4dd53243ae1161ba71)). Hades does grant real power, but because Darkness is persistent, "failed runs still contribute to long-term progress" ([games.gg](https://games.gg/de/hades/)). Vampire Survivors makes its stat PowerUps fully refundable, "so there's no risk in buying these upgrades" ([Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1794680/discussions/0/4631482569784832721)). Slay the Spire players do resent repeating equivalent proofs of skill, calling the per-character Ascension climb "extremely tedious" and "unnecessary grind" ([Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/1692659769943408705)).

The psychology literature backs this pattern and also limits it. The IKEA effect shows labor raises valuation, but "successful completion is an essential component": people who built and then destroyed their creations, or failed to finish them, showed no boost ([Norton, Mochon & Ariely, Harvard DASH](https://dash.harvard.edu/bitstreams/7312037d-2473-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b/download)). Effort pays off in value only when it ends in a finished result the player owns. Variable-ratio monetized rewards are the opposite of insight-weighted reward. Loot-box spending has a replicated but small association with problem-gambling severity (**η² ≈ 0.05**, n = 7,422 in the original) ([PLoS ONE 2018](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0206767); [PLoS ONE replication](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0213194&type=printable)). **No peer-reviewed study was found that directly measures how grind or time-gating affects satisfaction or churn.** The fairness conclusions here rest on convergent case evidence, not a controlled effect size.

## Permanent change pays off when losses feel meaningful and players can finish things

Opt-in permanence is a minority taste, but its players are highly engaged. **Only about 9% of Diablo III's 67.1M first-year characters were Hardcore** ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/diablo-3-one-year-anniversary-infographic-unleashes-hellishly-huge-numbers)), and **35% of Hardcore deaths happened in Act I Normal** ([GosuGamers](https://www.gosugamers.net/diablo/features/36947-game-design-updates-a-short-analysis)), which means permanence filters players out early. The research agrees on what makes loss acceptable: meaning. A 1,704-respondent survey found that deaths are judged negative mainly when they seem "meaningless" (Copcic et al., cited in [Carter & Allison, JGVW](https://www.doi.org/10.1386/jgvw.9.2.143_1)). In a 65-participant Don't Starve Together experiment, permadeath raised frustration, but the meaning players attributed to the death turned that frustration into willingness to keep playing ([NCCU](https://nccur.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/159388)). A 394-person study found that grief over lost characters increased appreciation of permadeath, especially among attached players ([WVU](https://researchrepository.wvu.edu/etd/7614)).

Large-scale, player-caused permanent destruction with a legible cause becomes celebrated history rather than a reason to quit. EVE Online's Battle of B-R5RB destroyed about 11 trillion ISK, including 75 Titans, worth roughly US$300k–330k. It was triggered by a missed sovereignty payment ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_B-R5RB); [UCSB Bottom Line](https://thebottomline.as.ucsb.edu/?p=20461)). Dwarf Fortress builds this in: "It's important that people learn to embrace loss, or the world can't be enriched by their passing" ([Technical.ly](https://technical.ly/brooklyn/2014/05/21/dwarf-fortress-adams/)), and world history persists across fortresses. Stardew Valley's Community Center and Baldur's Gate 3's reactive world turn permanent change into the arc of a finite game (synthesis). Helldivers 2's persistent Galactic War map is a shared version of the same idea.

Permanence also has costs that have to be designed around. Terraria's spreading Corruption, Crimson and Hallow force containment trenches 3–6 tiles wide ([Terraria Wiki](https://terraria.wiki.gg/Guide:Maintaining_world_purity)). Version 1.4's Journey Mode added a toggle that stops spread but cannot reverse it ([Terraria Wiki: Power Menu](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Power_Menu)), a shipped accessibility valve for irreversible world damage. Rust shows that periodic resets can drive retention. Facepunch forces monthly wipes, and RUSTalyzer data shows server population surging on wipe day and decaying afterward ([RUSTalyzer](https://www.rustalyzer.com/blog/server-wipe-quality-metrics)). In shared worlds, griefing is more widespread than expected ([AUT thesis](https://openrepository.aut.ac.nz/bitstream/handle/10292/12787/MatthewsB.pdf)). Save-scumming is under-researched ([BPS](https://bps.org.uk/news/save-scumming-role-playing-games-call-further-psychological-exploration)), and designers either build rewind into the game or make failure states interesting ([Game Developer on Shadow Gambit](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-shadow-gambit-the-cursed-crew-was-able-to-make-save-scumming-a-central-mechanic)). The IKEA result applies here directly: if a player's own dam or terraced farm is wiped out, the attachment their effort built can vanish with it. A ruinable world therefore needs a way to finish and preserve work, even while the live world stays fragile. **No study was found that measures whether persistent worlds or base-building raise retention, or how many players abandon a save after irreversible damage.**

## Rule discovery needs cheap tests, visible gaps and a reason to replay

Knowledge games show a common structure: a **near-zero-cost probe plus a legible change of state**. Examples are Baba Is You's free undo, Outer Wilds' 22-minute loop reset, and a single combine in Little Alchemy (synthesis). Designers deliberately let players get confused and then add breadcrumbs. Outer Wilds plants about three clues per "Curiosity" and avoids putting two rules for the same Curiosity on one planet ([Medium interview](https://medium.com/@cordialkobold/interview-with-alex-beachum-creative-director-of-outer-wilds-a01bb9631e20)). Its Ship Log "Rumor Mode" separates rumor facts from explored facts and shows how pieces of information relate ([Cuthbertson](https://paigecuthbertson.com/pdfs/The_Ship_Log_in_Outer_Wilds.pdf)). A "There's more to explore here" flag signals partial progress ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3500382207)). Jonathan Blow describes The Witness as "modelling the feeling of epiphany with great care" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-the-witness-i-modeling-epiphany)). Zachtronics made each SpaceChem puzzle "logically unique" so it "could not be solved by repeating a previous solution" ([SpaceChem postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-zachtronics-industries-i-spacechem-i-)). This lines up with information-gap theory, under which MDA's Discovery aesthetic is "the pleasure of reducing information gaps through the exploratory actions a player takes" ([DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/793/793/790)). A gap only motivates once the player notices it and believes it can be closed.

Combination-discovery games reach huge audiences but drift toward brute force. Infinite Craft's launch post drew 15M+ views in a week, and players tried 200,000 unique combinations on launch day ([Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/sites/infinite-craft); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/tips/how-many-possible-combinations-are-there-in-infinite-craft-answered)). Doodle God's 3D edition has 5M+ Google Play installs ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=joybits.doodlegod_hd_blitz&hl=en_US)). Potion Craft sold 100,000 in three days ([VGChartz](https://www.vgchartz.com/article/450825/potion-craft-sales-top-100000-in-3-days-on-steam/)). When combinations are arbitrary, discovery becomes enumeration, which is time effort dressed up as insight. Results that can be predicted from underlying properties are what make recipes inferable rather than memorized. That is the natural advantage of a sandbox built on physics-style primitives. Minecraft eventually added an auto-unlocking recipe book in 1.12 (2017) ([Craftdex](https://craftdex.net/articles/the-recipe-book-and-unlocks)), a sign that even a hugely successful discovery game needed a recording layer to keep the joy of discovery without the chore of memorizing.

Pure knowledge games have a replay problem. Outer Wilds players wish they "could wipe [their] memory of it" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/753640/discussions/0/598531176428279474/)), and with full knowledge the end credits are reachable in under 30 minutes ([Steam "Replay value?"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/753640/discussions/0/2518023667589574178/)). Beachum originally imagined the game as a roguelike with randomized elements ([The Fourth Curtain podcast](https://open.spotify.com/episode/0iuocjlbq4hWDV7B0y3smJ)). Three remedies work. One is optimization histograms: Zachtronics' culture began when players competed in Kongregate comments to post the most efficient solutions ([SpaceChem postmortem](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-zachtronics-industries-i-spacechem-i-)), later formalized in Opus Magnum and Exapunks ([Exapunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exapunks)). Another is layered community meta-secrets, as in Fez, Tunic and Animal Well ([Game Developer on Animal Well](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/why-animal-well-s-home-brewed-engine-was-key-to-its-success)). The third is per-world rule randomization.

Randomized rules have a long, mixed track record that bears directly on the planned mode. **Noita randomizes only two alchemy recipes per seed**, each made of three materials ([Noita Wiki: Alchemy](https://noita.wiki.gg/wiki/Alchemy)), and players quickly built seed-to-recipe calculators ([Neff's recipe generator](https://neffc.github.io/narg/); [Zatherz](https://zatherz.github.io/noita/)). Rogue and NetHack reshuffle item appearances every game. Identification has to stay scarce enough that waiting has a cost ([Game Developer @Play](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-play-81-on-rogue-s-item-identification-system)). Players love the moment of drinking an unknown potion on their last hitpoint and surviving, but hate price-identification and workaround rituals that become "spoilers and tedium… game after game". One proposed fix is to show a small set of possible identities on inspection ([Golden Krone Hotel](https://www.goldenkronehotel.com/wp/2017/06/25/things-i-hate-about-roguelikes-part-2-identification/); [NetHack idea tracker](https://nethack-yanis.github.io/tag/identification)). The lesson for the planned mode is to randomize **constants**, keep **methods** valuable, and give players instruments for measuring the world so relearning feels like science rather than ritual.

## Four frameworks point to the same audience

The four frameworks the research plan names converge on one profile: players who find tinkering, deduction and authorship intrinsically rewarding and who need to feel competent and autonomous. MDA's eight aesthetics give the vocabulary: Sensation, Fantasy, Narrative, Challenge, Fellowship, Discovery, Expression and Submission ([MDA paper](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf)). But MDA names the target emotion, not its motivational cause, and has no measurement instrument ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/mda-framework-unconnected-connectivity)). Quantic Foundry's model has **12 motivations in 6 pairs** and draws on more than 1.5 million self-selected respondents. It places Design and Discovery together in the Creativity cluster ([Handy reference chart](https://quanticfoundry.com/2015/12/15/handy-reference/); [PMC review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9872527)). Two of its findings matter here. Strategy is the most age-stable motivation (r ≈ −.05 to .06 with age), but it has also declined the most since 2015: **the average 2024 gamer sits at about the 33rd percentile of the 2015 distribution** ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2024/05/21/strategy-decline/)). Nick Yee suggests that hybrids such as Balatro, which pair strategy with surprise and novelty, are how designers can respond ([Player Driven](https://playerdriven.substack.com/p/quantic-foundrys-nick-yee-on-gamer)). The sample is 74% male and the genre-level scores are behind a paywall ([Evolution of Play](https://quanticfoundry.com/evolution-of-play/); [Motivation Insight Report](https://quanticfoundry.com/insight-report/)), so this report does not claim genre rankings.

Bartle's 1996 four types come from one designer's observation of MUD players ([Bartle](https://mud.co.uk/richard/hcds.htm)). Yee's factor analysis of about 3,000 MMO players replaced them with three components (Achievement, Social, Immersion). Crucially, it found that **the "Explorer" splits into mapping the world versus understanding the mechanics**, and that achievement and competition correlate at r = .41, against Bartle's separation of Achievers and Killers ([Yee, "Revisiting Bartle's Types"](https://nickyee.com/facets/revisiting.html)). Self-determination theory's PENS model found, across four studies, that in-game autonomy and competence predicted enjoyment, future play and changes in well-being, and that relatedness also predicted outcomes in multiplayer communities ([Ryan, Rigby & Przybylski 2006](https://www.rochester.edu/warner/lida/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/02bfe513dd59366750000000.pdf); [PENS](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/)). Effect sizes could not be extracted and are not quoted. The table maps the three groups and the three pillars onto the frameworks. **The mappings are synthesis.**

| | MDA primary aesthetics | Quantic Foundry motivations | Yee component | SDT need |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Systemic / sandbox group | Discovery, Expression, Challenge | Design, Discovery, Strategy (Destruction for Teardown/Noita) | Immersion: Discovery, Customization; Achievement: Mechanics | Autonomy, competence |
| Knowledge / discovery group | Discovery, Challenge, Narrative | Discovery, Strategy, Story | Achievement: Mechanics (understanding) | Competence |
| Commercial control group | Challenge, Fellowship, Sensation, Fantasy | Competition, Excitement, Power, Completion, Fantasy | Achievement: Advancement, Competition; Social | Competence, relatedness |
| Pillar 1: insight-weighted reward | Challenge (cognitive) | Strategy, Challenge (away from Completion and Power) | Mechanics | Competence |
| Pillar 2: permanent world change | Expression, Discovery, Submission | Design, Destruction | Customization | Autonomy |
| Pillar 3: rule discovery and recipes | Discovery, Expression | Discovery, Strategy, Design | Mechanics-understanding explorer | Competence, autonomy (relatedness if shared) |

The design gap is clear from this table: **relatedness is unaddressed unless recipes can be shared**, and every long-lived systemic game in the study has a sharing layer (blueprints, Workshop mods, TotK build sites, Powder Toy saves, redstone designs). Quantic Foundry's free profile and a short PENS-style questionnaire give a practical way to segment playtesters by Creativity and Mastery scores and to track competence and autonomy per build ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/); [PENS](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/player-experience-of-needs-satisfaction-pens/)).

## Recommendations tied to the three pillars

The current design (a 2D falling-sand grid; eight primitives with conserved heat; five verbs; an auto-recording journal; nestable recipes; a randomized-constants mode with a headless viability check) already reflects most of the evidence. The recommendations below sharpen it. Each is tied to a pillar, the evidence behind it, and a metric the prototype can log, in line with section 6 of `02-ruleset-mvp.md`. They are hypotheses to test in the prototype, not settled facts.

| # | Pillar | Recommendation | Evidence | Prototype metric |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | P1, P3 | Guarantee a first player-made recipe within the first session, well inside the 6.6 h median before negative reviews. Seed the starting area with two or three observable but unexplained phenomena (e.g. steam condensing on cold stone). | Negative reviews at 6.6 h median; design complaints at 57% of negative reviews ([PC Games Insider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/news/67005/report-bad-design-is-the-no1-complaint-of-steam-user-review-authors)); onboarding cliff in Factorio, Noita, Cultist Simulator | Time to first observed reaction; time to first saved recipe |
| 2 | P3 | Turn the journal into a rumor/verified log: auto-record unexplained observations as "?" entries and confirmed reactions as laws, with a "more to discover here" flag. | Outer Wilds Rumor Mode and partial-progress flag ([Cuthbertson](https://paigecuthbertson.com/pdfs/The_Ship_Log_in_Outer_Wilds.pdf)); information-gap theory ([DiGRA](https://dl.digra.org/index.php/dl/article/download/793/793/790)) | Share of "?" entries the player deliberately tests; observation-to-law time |
| 3 | P1 | Keep probes cheap: small-scale experiments should cost little and be easy to undo or contain. Reserve risk and conserved-energy cost for large-scale application. | Free undo in Baba Is You; loop reset in Outer Wilds; Noita's "no safety net" complaint ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/noita-review/)) | Experiments per hour; abandonment after a failed experiment |
| 4 | P1 | Make recipes the grind-to-insight converter: once written, a recipe runs as one action and can be scaled. Score recipes on yield, energy and time, and show the player's result against a distribution. | Factorio blueprints; TotK Autobuild; Zachtronics histograms ([Exapunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exapunks)) | Reward per unit effort by type; recipe re-use rate; share of recipes revised for efficiency |
| 5 | P1 | Never devalue past effort. Rebalance by adding rules, not by nerfing recipes players already own; when a randomized world breaks a recipe, mark it "unverified here" rather than deleting it. | Diablo IV Season 14 backlash ([DiabloBytes](https://www.diablobytes.com/diablo-iv/news/)); Destiny 2 redesign ([Hitmarker](https://hitmarker.net/news/destiny-2s-latest-expansion-failed-to-meet-player-expectations-director-admits-1586995)) | Sentiment tags for "lost progress" in Early Access reviews |
| 6 | P1 | Make persistent unlocks widen what the player can do (instrument precision, new verbs such as observing temperature) rather than raise stats; any power boosts should be small and refundable. | Balatro and Slay the Spire breadth unlocks; Vampire Survivors refunds ([Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1794680/discussions/0/4631482569784832721)) | Ratio of capability unlocks to stat unlocks |
| 7 | P1 | Do not gate the expressive toolkit behind luck. Variability should be informational (natural noise that teaches the rules), never monetized. | Noita wand-luck complaints ([GOG](https://www.gog.com/game/noita)); loot-box/gambling association ([PLoS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0206767)) | Variance in time to first key capability across seeds |
| 8 | P2 | Make large-scale damage legible and caused by the player: trace collapses back to the reactions and energy flows that caused them, so loss becomes insight. | Losses judged negative when "meaningless" ([Carter & Allison](https://www.doi.org/10.1386/jgvw.9.2.143_1)); EVE's legible trigger | Post-collapse continuation rate; "why did that happen" journal lookups |
| 9 | P2 | Let players finish and preserve work in a fragile world: snapshots, a "fossil record" or museum of past states, and named landmarks that persist in the journal even after the cells are destroyed. | IKEA effect vanishes when creations are destroyed ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=1777100)) | Snapshot use; return rate after self-caused destruction |
| 10 | P2 | Make spreading damage slow and visible, give players containment tools, and offer an optional "pause spread" setting. Ruined areas should create new ecology to discover, not just dead ground. | Terraria spread and Journey Mode toggle ([Terraria Wiki](https://terraria.wiki.gg/wiki/Power_Menu)); Dwarf Fortress "Losing is fun" | World-change score (cells changed × persistence); save-abandonment rate |
| 11 | P2, P3 | Use expansions that add primitives (electricity, pressure, wind, magnetism are already deferred) as the long-term re-engagement engine, not content packs. | Factorio 3.4x and Satisfactory 3.1x late peaks ([GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2024/10/factorio-space-age-sold-over-400000-copies-and-sets-a-new-player-count-record); [Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2024/09/11/satisfactory-peaks-at-over-100k-ccu-launch)) | Lapsed-player return after each primitive update |
| 12 | P3 (random mode) | Offer tiered randomization: a default that perturbs a few high-value constants (Noita randomizes only two recipes) and a full mode for experts. Keep primitives, conservation and verbs fixed, as D2 already specifies. | Noita alchemy ([Noita Wiki](https://noita.wiki.gg/wiki/Alchemy)); NetHack ID tedium ([Golden Krone Hotel](https://www.goldenkronehotel.com/wp/2017/06/25/things-i-hate-about-roguelikes-part-2-identification/)) | Completion rate and session count per randomization tier |
| 13 | P3 (random mode) | Replace identification rituals with in-world instruments (thermometer, scale, sample burner) and a "possible values" readout that narrows as the player measures, so relearning feels like doing science. | Golden Krone "possible identities" proposal; NetHack price-ID critique | Measurements per new world; time to re-verify core recipes |
| 14 | P3 (random mode) | Decide deliberately whether seeds are shareable. If they are, expect community calculators within days and treat them as engagement; if relearning is the point, keep variation unshareable. | Noita seed-to-recipe tools ([Neff](https://neffc.github.io/narg/)) | Share of players using external tools (survey) |
| 15 | P3 | Add sharing of recipes (export/import, possibly verified-in-world badges) to supply the missing SDT relatedness need and to turn individual insight into community knowledge. | TotK build communities ([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2024/04/02/tears-of-the-kingdom-creation-boasts-games-longest-object/)); Infinite Craft "First Discovery" ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/is-infinite-craft-actually-infinite/)) | Recipes shared and imported per active player |
| 16 | All | Pair deduction with spectacle: a successful insight should produce a visible, satisfying change in the world, not a quiet unlock. | Strategy motivation at the 33rd percentile of its 2015 level; Yee's Balatro hybrid point ([Player Driven](https://playerdriven.substack.com/p/quantic-foundrys-nick-yee-on-gamer)) | PENS competence score after insight moments |

Two of these change the design docs, not just the tuning. Recommendation 12 qualifies D2's "learn reality from the ground up every time", which is best kept as the expert tier rather than the default. Recommendations 9 and 15 add preservation and sharing systems that the MVP currently lists as placeholders (saving beyond a single world; multiplayer). The conserved-quantity foundation already answers the steelman objection to Pillar 1, that a clever exploit is disproportionate reward. Leverage that spends stored potential is earned insight, and spending it changes the world. The evidence above supports that framing, since players' fairness complaints are about devaluation and opacity, not about clever players winning big.

## What is sourced, what is synthesis, and what is still unknown

The **sourced** material is the live Steam review and Steam Charts figures for 37 games (2026-10-20), the remaining sales, concurrent-player and review figures (press- or tracker-reported and mostly 2024–2025), the developer design statements, the player quotes, and the academic findings. The **synthesis** is every loop breakdown in the report and the CSV (core action through new capability, time scale, dominant effort type), every MDA and Quantic Foundry tag per game, the framework-to-pillar mapping, the theme tally, the "24 h peak ÷ all-time peak" long-tail proxy, and the causal reading that new rule sets drive late peaks. Several items in the notes were flagged as not re-verified against the primary text: MDA's worked game examples, Yee's ten subcomponent names and Quantic Foundry's three-bridge structure. They are omitted or used only for framing. Missing data includes current figures for games that are not on Steam or were not in the pull (Minecraft, the Zelda games, The Powder Toy, Sandboxels, Fortnite, League of Legends, Genshin Impact and others); the dates of Steam Charts all-time peaks that press coverage does not report (e.g. Satisfactory's 185,957); Steam playtime, since SteamSpy's playtime fields were empty; official sales for Noita, Oxygen Not Included and Outer Wilds; any D1/D7/D30 curve for a premium PC game; any controlled study of grind or of world persistence on retention; the extracted PC figures from GameAnalytics' 2026 benchmarks; and the contents of the Noita and Outer Wilds GDC talks. SteamDB, HowLongToBeat, Metacritic and Quantic Foundry pages were still inaccessible. The next step is to extend the existing live pull from aggregate scores to full review text for these 37 app IDs: cursor pagination at 100 reviews per page ([Apify](https://apify.com/venturesome_laugh/steam-reviews-scraper.md)), BERTopic clustering, which beat LDA and NMF on coherence for Steam reviews ([University of Hertfordshire](https://researchprofiles.herts.ac.uk/en/publications/unravelling-players-insights-a-comparative-analysis-of-topic-mode/)), LLM labelling with a fixed codebook, and validation against about 300 hand-coded reviews.

## Conclusion

The control group shows that durability does not come from a large amount of content. It comes from a reward that does not run out. Competitive games get that from opponents; systemic games get it from rules whose combinations exceed what their designers imagined, as Nintendo said of Ultrahand builds ([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2024/06/05/tears-of-the-kingdom-director-and-producer-reflect-on-the-games-first-year/)). A natural-science sandbox built on conserved quantities and local rules is structurally in the second camp. That is a stronger position than either a finite content game or a pure knowledge game, because the planned randomized-constants mode answers the replay problem that caps Outer Wilds. The design risk sits elsewhere: at the start, where opaque rules lose players before their first insight, and in the middle, where players must be able to finish and keep what their effort made in a world that can be ruined.

The fairness evidence also changes the meaning of "reward proportional to effort". Players are not measuring reward against hours. They are measuring whether the game respects what they have already learned and built. The worst failures in the study come from breaking that contract: nerfs, resets, progression redesigns, and luck that blocks access to tools. The conserved-energy foundation, never-deleted recipes, and preserved records of past world states are all ways of keeping that contract. They should be tested in the prototype as directly as the question of whether discovery is fun.
